# Evidence-led review

## Source map

Record enough provenance to keep rewrites honest; a compact internal table is sufficient.

| ID | Source/location | Exact fact or quote | Context | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Resume, employer A, bullet 2 | Exact original text | Production / lab / advisory / unspecified | Candidate-stated |
| U1 | User clarification | Confirmed scope or result | Role and period | Candidate-confirmed |
| J1 | JD, required qualifications | Exact requirement including AND/OR | Target job | Employer-stated |
| I1 | Reviewer analysis | Plausible role direction | Target assumption only | Inferred |

Candidate-stated means supplied, not independently verified. Do not demand proof of every ordinary resume statement. Seek clarification when a material ambiguity, contradiction, or proposed expansion matters. User corrections may supersede the resume; retain the mapping. Keep facts attached to the correct employer and period.

## Review dimensions

- **Document:** Can the original file be opened? Is the text present and in usable order? Is the application format known? Separate extraction observations from visual observations.
- **First impression:** What role and strongest relevant evidence are visible near the start? A text excerpt does not reproduce a rendered top third.
- **Evidence:** What did the candidate personally do? What did the team do? What was delivered, changed, maintained, or learned? Distinguish missing description from missing experience.
- **Fit:** Compare central responsibilities and level, not just product names. Distinguish requirements, alternatives, preferences, and unresolved eligibility.
- **Credibility:** Check internal consistency and technical meaning. State an ambiguity and its resolution; do not infer dishonesty.

## Finding format

Use **priority + source quote/location + observation + implication + concrete fix**. Add confidence or an evidence limitation where it changes the conclusion. Keep the four review lenses as optional tags; one finding need not be repeated under every lens.

Example: “High: ‘Created dashboards and reports for leadership’ names a deliverable but not its purpose. Keep that statement; ask what the reports covered and which decisions they informed. Do not change ‘leadership’ to ‘board’ without confirmation.”

## Output proportionality

A quick review is a short verdict, a few exact edits, and essential questions. A comprehensive review can add requirement mapping, scored categories, and technical detail. A rewrite request should result in a rewrite, with a brief explanation instead of an unsolicited second report. Omit learning advice when the issue is writing rather than a demonstrated learning gap.

Use separate confidence statements for content, target fit, and document format. A complete text resume can support high-confidence wording edits while leaving layout unassessed. Do not downgrade every dimension because only one is unavailable.

## Completion

Check that quotes are exact; combined quotes and paraphrases are labeled. Preserve valid specifics and appropriately bounded verbs. Count experience time only from relevant periods, handle overlaps without double-counting, and state month-level uncertainty when dates contain only years. Never turn years in IT into the same number of years in security, cloud, or leadership.
